\chapter{Final Remarks}\label{ch:final_remarks}

\section{Conclusions}

This thesis has presented a novel approach to address the challenges
of Image Semantic Segmentation (ISS) in histopathology images,
particularly in crowdsourcing scenarios where multiple annotators
with varying levels of expertise contribute to the labeling process.
The proposed solution successfully tackles the fundamental challenges
of modeling annotator performance and handling label inconsistencies
while maintaining computational efficiency. The work demonstrates
significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods,
particularly in scenarios with noisy and missing labels, which are
common in crowdsourcing approaches.

Throughout this work, several techniques have been explored to develop a
model that can face \gls{ISS} of real world histopathology images in
crowdsourcing scenarios. Different approaches were studied and evaluated
to reach an optimal solution which could model the annotators' performance
from the input space. The following remarks are based on the results
obtained from the experiments conducted and reviewed literature:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Chained Gaussian Processes approaches, while robust for classification
    and regression tasks, proved computationally intensive for image
    segmentation.
    This limitation highlighted the need for more efficient
    approaches that could
    handle the complexity of medical image segmentation while modeling annotator
    performance.
  \item The development of a novel loss function was crucial for the success
    of this work, as existing loss functions were insufficient to model the
    annotators' performance across the input space. The proposed loss function
    successfully enabled the model to adaptively infer the best possible
    segmentation without requiring separate inputs about labeler performance.
  \item The introduction of a tensor map to codify labeler reliability proved
    to be an effective approach, allowing the model to implicitly weigh the
    labelers based on their performance across the mask and classes space,
    thus handling the labeler performance inconsistency effectively.
  \item The use of U-Shaped Deep Learning models as a building block
    for the proposed solution was validated, as it effectively captured
    spatial relationships between pixels and annotations while handling
    the variability in image quality.
  \item The combination of Chained Deep Learning approaches with the
    proposed loss function and tensor map representation resulted in a
    comprehensive solution being, to the best of our knowledge, the
    first approach on offering an image segmentation solution for
    multiple labelers in medical image with interpretability in the
    reliability of the labelers, successfully addressing both
    labeler inconsistency and image quality variability. The model outperformed
    other state of the art solutions in terms of segmentation
    accuracy in noisy and missing labels conditions, which is rather
    common in crowdsourcing approaches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}

As tools and frameworks for bayesian approaches like Chained Gaussian Processes
\footnote{Including GPFlow, and GPFlux.}
evolve, it is expected to see a decrease in computational
requirements and hence, making mixed models more appealing for the
task of \gls{ISS} of histopathology images.
In that sense, the use of \gls{CCGPMA} as a building block for the
proposed solution could be a good approach, as it is a powerful model
that can take advantage of the inter-dependencies between the annotators,
and the powerfulness of \glspl{CNN} to extract features from the images.

The noisy labelers emulation technique developed in this work presents
an opportunity to test the model's robustness in various medical image
segmentation challenges. This technique could be adapted to simulate
different types of expert variability in other medical imaging domains:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Multi-modal Medical Imaging}: The technique could be
    extended to simulate expert disagreements in multi-modal scenarios,
    where different imaging modalities (e.g., MRI, CT, PET) might lead
    to varying interpretations of the same anatomical structure.
  \item \textbf{Temporal Consistency}: For longitudinal studies, the
    emulation could be modified to simulate how expert annotations
    might vary over time, helping to test the model's ability to
    maintain consistency in segmentation across different time points.
  \item \textbf{Cross-institutional Variability}: The noise injection
    technique could be calibrated to simulate the variations in
    annotation patterns that arise from different institutional
    protocols or training backgrounds.
  \item \textbf{Pathology-specific Challenges}: The emulation could be
    tailored to simulate expert disagreements in specific pathological
    conditions, where the morphological features might be more
    ambiguous or subject to interpretation.
\end{itemize}

These extensions would help validate the model's performance across a
broader range of medical imaging scenarios and ensure its robustness
in real-world clinical applications.

The pixel-wise reliability mapping approach shows particular promise
for future development. While the current implementation already
demonstrates good performance, several enhancements could lead to
significant improvements:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Advanced Data Augmentation}: Implementing a comprehensive
    data augmentation strategy could significantly improve the model's
    convergence and performance. This could include:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Geometric transformations (rotations, flips, elastic deformations)
      \item Intensity variations to simulate different staining conditions
      \item Multi-scale training to handle varying tissue sizes
      \item Adversarial training to improve robustness
    \end{itemize}
    These augmentations would help the model learn more robust features
    and better generalize to unseen data.
  \item \textbf{Attention Mechanisms}: Integrating attention mechanisms
    into the pixel-wise reliability branch could help the model focus
    on the most relevant regions for reliability estimation, potentially
    improving both accuracy and computational efficiency.
  \item \textbf{Multi-scale Reliability Estimation}: Implementing a
    hierarchical approach to reliability estimation could capture
    both fine-grained local variations and broader regional patterns
    in annotator reliability, this would imply a network with yet
    more branches and outputs.
\end{itemize}

The combination of these enhancements with the existing pixel-wise
reliability mapping approach could potentially outperform all other
techniques, as it would provide the most detailed and accurate
representation of annotator reliability while maintaining computational
efficiency. The use of data augmentation, in particular, could help
the model converge faster and achieve better generalization, as it
would expose the model to a wider variety of possible variations in
both the input images and the corresponding annotations.

\section{Academic Discussion}\label{sec:academic_discussion}

To facilitate the adoption and further development of the proposed approach,
several resources have been made publicly available. A comprehensive GitHub
repository \footnote{https://github.com/blotero/seg\_tgce} has been
created that provides easy-to-use APIs for implementing
the proposed solution. The repository includes pre-built dataset loaders for
TensorFlow/Keras frameworks, implementation of the TGCE$_{SS}$ loss function
in all its variants (scalar, features, and pixel-wise), and a neural network
architecture builder with experimental examples.
The two \textit{tfds} datasets available in the repository are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Triple Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) Dataset}: A dataset of
    histopathology images of breast cancer, with annotations of the tumor
    regions from \cite{LopezEtAl2024}.
    The dataset is automatically fetched from AWS S3, which
    eliminates the need for manual downloads or external dependencies.
  \item \textbf{Oxford-IIIT Pet Dataset}: A dataset of pet
    images, based on the well-known Oxford-IIIT Pet Dataset with
    annotations of the pet regions.
    The mechanism for emulating noisy crowdsourced annotations
    exposed throughout the present work (see Section
    \ref{sec:noisy_annotations_generation}) is also available in the
    repository in a tunable manner, just as the TNBC dataset, this
    \texttt{tfds} is batteries included (automatic fetching and
    mapping, no need to manually download any external source).
\end{itemize}

To further simplify the adoption of the proposed solution, a PyPI package
has been published with detailed documentation available at
\url{https://seg-tgce.readthedocs.io/en/latest/}. The documentation
provides comprehensive installation instructions, API reference, usage
examples, and best practices for implementation. This package makes it
straightforward for the community to integrate the proposed approach into
their existing workflows, with a focus on ease of use and clear
documentation of all available features and options.

An academic publication is currently being prepared for submission to a Q1
journal. This publication will provide detailed theoretical foundations of
the proposed approach, comprehensive experimental results and analysis,
comparison with state-of-the-art methods, and discussion of limitations
and future directions. The publication will serve as the primary reference
for the theoretical and practical aspects of the proposed solution,
ensuring that the work is properly documented and validated through the
peer-review process.

These resources collectively contribute to the reproducibility and
accessibility of the research, enabling the broader scientific community
to build upon and extend the proposed approach. The combination of
open-source code, well-documented APIs, and peer-reviewed publication
ensures that the work can be properly evaluated, validated, and
utilized by other researchers in the field. This comprehensive approach
to sharing research outputs aligns with current best practices in
scientific computing and machine learning research, promoting both
transparency and practical utility of the proposed solution.
